I came into biology through physics and complex systems, so I still have a reflex to ask what is interacting with what, on which topology, and with what stability.

That reflex remains useful even when the system refuses to look like a clean graph.

<Sidenote mark="partial map">
  Most of the graph is never measured (we see a few edges and guess the rest).
</Sidenote>

A cell has thousands of genes and we only ever measure part of what they are doing. Still, words like coupling and perturbation carry over, and they remind me that a small local change can shift what the whole system does, in ways no single measurement would show.

I don't think every problem should be forced into a network. What I want to keep is the intuition that structure and trajectories matter, and that how well a state holds up under a push is often the real quantity of interest.

That is the thread from my older work in nonlinear dynamics to genomics now. I keep looking for a description simple enough to reason with, without throwing away the parts that make it true.
