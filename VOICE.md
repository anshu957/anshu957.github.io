# Voice

Notes on Anshul's writing voice, drawn from the old blog *solitarydisposition — reflections from solitude* (posts 2013–2020). The goal is **not** to reproduce that younger, rawer voice. It's to keep a few **faint, deliberate traces** of it in the current, more mature site writing — a glimmer under the surface, not the whole confessional.

## The through-line (this is the important one)

He reaches for **physics and dynamical systems to describe inner life**, instinctively and unpretentiously. Not as decoration — it's genuinely how he thinks. Evidence from the old posts:

- A friend's stuck life described as "locally a straight line with monotonic growth but on a higher scale becomes a circle" — then pushed further: magnify the scale, "does it become a point?"
- Order vs. chaos weighed like a stability analysis: order is "cozy, comforting but vulnerable," chaos "uncomforting but highly robust." (He already used *robust* about a state's resilience.)
- Human connections as "self-sustained… perpetual motion in physicist language."
- The obsession with **continuity / discontinuity** — a story needs continuity, death is the discontinuity he can't make peace with.

This is the seam where the old voice and the current site (cell-fate attractors, basins, phase space) are the *same person*. When a science metaphor on the site quietly carries a second meaning about impermanence, meaning, or being stuck — that's the core voice showing through. Use this sparingly and it will never read as forced, because it's real.

## Core traits (durable)

- **Introspective and existential.** Recurring preoccupations: death, continuity, meaning, impermanence, "the finishing point," the fear of having a thing "only to be taken away few moments later." Melancholy as a considered stance, not a mood.
- **Honest and self-undercutting.** Says the heavy thing plainly, then steps back and examines it almost clinically. Qualifies his own claims mid-sentence — "(whatever that means)," "it could be delusional," "I'm not depressed in any way." Never melodramatic; the honesty *is* the effect.
- **Questioning, dialectical, comfortable with irresolution.** Lays out choices ([a] jump into chaos / [b] impose order), turns an idea over, and leaves it unresolved on purpose. He trusts the reader to sit in the open question.
- **Solitude as posture.** The blog's whole premise. Values authentic, "random" individual connection over obligatory community. Quiet, inward, unhurried.
- **Reaches for poetry and citation** when prose runs out — Schrödinger, Shiv Kumar Batalvi, a film lyric, Punjabi verse with his own translation. Literary and cross-lingual (English + Hindi/Punjabi).

## Sentence-level habits

*Keep, in trace amounts:* the trailing thought that opens into a question rather than closing; the parenthetical aside that undercuts or qualifies; a plain-spoken heavy sentence set against a light one; the occasional quiet first-person admission of not-knowing.

*Dial down now (youthful rawness he's evolved past):* clouds of ellipses "….", lowercase "i", casual profanity, unbounded run-ons, raw despair. The mature writing should feel **composed** — the melancholy filtered into restraint, not spilled.

## How to apply it on this site

The site is science-facing and professional. The voice should be a **glimmer**, surfacing maybe once per piece:

- A dynamical-systems image that's secretly also about something human (a basin you can't leave; a trajectory that never settles; continuity and its breaks).
- One honest sentence admitting what isn't known or resolved, instead of papering over it.
- A single quiet personal aside in an otherwise rigorous paragraph — then back to the work.
- An epigraph or a line of verse where it genuinely fits, not as ornament.
- Comfort ending on an open question rather than a tidy conclusion.

**Litmus test:** if a line could have appeared, in more composed form, in the old blog *and* belongs in a research notebook — that's the voice. If it's just wistful for its own sake, cut it.

## Quick contrast

- Off-voice (generic academic): "In this work we present a robust framework for characterizing cell-fate transitions."
- On-voice (a glimmer): "A cell at a decision point is briefly in neither basin — and that in-between, the part that resists a clean label, is the part I keep coming back to."

Same rigor; one quiet seam of the person underneath.
